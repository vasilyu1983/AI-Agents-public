# Primitive: Neuroaesthetics

## Definition

Neuroaesthetics studies aesthetic experience and its neural correlates. Peak-shift is a proposed explanatory principle; symmetry and contour preferences vary with stimuli and people. These research ideas motivate candidate designs, not a claim that a specific landing page produces ventral reward or that exaggerating a brand signal necessarily improves conversion.

## Evidence boundary

Apply [the evidence-to-product contract](../../../references/evidence-to-product.md) before a mechanism claim. Bounce, cancellation, eye tracking and clicks do not diagnose neural circuitry. Distinguish observed behavior, candidate explanations and measured mechanisms. Neural claims require valid distinct measurements, selectivity and base-rate reasoning, plus intervention evidence for a design prescription. Do not invent timing rules or a neuroscience-derived uplift.

## When to use

Use for an explicit aesthetic research question or testing visual alternatives. Routine hierarchy and accessibility decisions can remain with design/usability skills.

## Inputs and outputs

Record audience, task, competing visual variants and functional promises. Return a local comparison plan with preference, credibility, comprehension, task completion and accessibility measures. Keep explanations behavioral unless neural evidence is actually collected and validated.

## Failure modes

| Observation | Alternatives to investigate | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Beautiful page, high bounce | Irrelevant offer, slow load, poor navigation, misleading acquisition traffic | Test relevance and task completion; no reward diagnosis or mandatory three-second rule |
| Signups rise but refunds increase | Promise mismatch, pricing misunderstanding, poor first-use quality | Compare promise comprehension and first-use outcomes |
| Generic visual identity | Low distinctiveness or audience mismatch | Test identity alternatives without assuming peak-shift superiority |
| Team claims neuroscience design | Mechanism or intervention evidence absent | Report design/usability rationale; naming a principle is insufficient |

## Worked example

An app's premium-looking redesign increases initial conversion but lowers trial satisfaction. These observations are consistent with expectation mismatch, among other explanations. Interview users, measure promise comprehension, compare truthful variants and inspect functional quality. Do not infer a neural reward response from conversion or dissatisfaction. Avoid implying quality the product cannot deliver.

## Sources

- Ramachandran & Hirstein (1999), The science of art, Journal of Consciousness Studies 6(6–7): proposed neurological account, not a tested landing-page intervention.
- Chatterjee (2014), The Aesthetic Brain: research synthesis, not a conversion guarantee.
- [Poldrack (2011), Inferring mental states from neuroimaging data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240863/): reverse-inference limitations.
