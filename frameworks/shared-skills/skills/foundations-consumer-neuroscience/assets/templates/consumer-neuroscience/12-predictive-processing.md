# Primitive: Predictive Processing & Active Inference

## Definition

Predictive processing is a family of computational accounts of perception and action. In a specified probabilistic model, surprisal is negative log probability; variational free energy bounds surprisal; prediction error depends on model predictions; precision weights uncertainty (often inverse variance). These quantities are distinct. Unexpected input is not automatically high precision, and precision is not diagnosed from anxiety or learned interface familiarity.

## Evidence boundary

Apply [the evidence-to-product contract](../../../references/evidence-to-product.md) before a mechanism claim. Bounce, cancellation, eye tracking and clicks do not diagnose neural circuitry. Distinguish observed behavior, candidate explanations and measured mechanisms. Neural claims require valid distinct measurements, selectivity and base-rate reasoning, plus intervention evidence for a design prescription. Do not invent timing rules or a neuroscience-derived uplift.

## When to use

Use for an explicit calibrated computational model or as a carefully labeled hypothesis about user expectations during product changes. Ordinary usability change evaluation does not require a neural explanation.

## Inputs and outputs

Record expected interface behavior, planned change, user reports, alternatives and task measures. Return a qualitative expectation map and tests of comprehension, friction, task completion and satisfaction. Only return quantitative model costs or precision estimates when an explicit fitted model, measurement method and validation support them. There is no default prediction-error or attentional budget for product surprises.

## Failure modes

| Observation | Alternatives to investigate | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Confusion after update | Unclear labels, changed workflow, content mismatch | Compare notice, explanation and user control |
| Reveal generates anxiety | Pressure, ambiguity, unmet needs | Collect reports and test clearer alternatives; no high-precision diagnosis |
| Adoption falls | Changed task fit, visibility, onboarding friction | Inspect task outcomes and compare variants |

## Worked example

Illustrative app replaces its short Sunday card with a long reading; engagement falls 40% and users report confusion. This suggests a possible expectation or task-fit mismatch, not a measured precision state or neural cost. Test clear explanation, an optional longer reading and continued short-card access. Choose advance notice from audience needs and local testing; no prescribed four-week priming. Compare comprehension and usage over matched windows while accounting for novelty and user mix.

## Ethical boundary

Preserve user control and truthful explanations; do not use uncertainty or unpredictability to manufacture pressure or compulsive engagement.
