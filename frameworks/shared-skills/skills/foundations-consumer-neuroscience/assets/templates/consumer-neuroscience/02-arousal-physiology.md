# Primitive: Arousal Physiology

## Definition

Arousal is the dimension of physiological activation running from deep sleep to peak stress. Two canonical mechanisms govern it in consumer contexts:

1. **Yerkes-Dodson inverted-U** (Yerkes & Dodson, 1908): Performance and engagement quality peak at moderate arousal and degrade at both extremes. For complex tasks (reading, evaluating offers, making decisions), the optimal arousal is lower than for simple tasks. Exceeding optimal arousal shifts the user from engagement to stress without improving performance.

2. **Autonomic nervous system markers**: Arousal activates the sympatho-adrenal axis — rising heart rate, skin conductance (GSR), and cortisol. GSR is the primary real-time marker used in neuromarketing research; it indicates activation but does not distinguish positive excitement from negative stress. McEwen's allostatic load model (2007) establishes that sustained elevated autonomic activation — even in a "positive" product context — is a genuine physiological cost. The Frontiers in Neuroergonomics systematic review (2025) confirms GSR as the most widely used peripheral arousal measure across consumer buying stages, with the multimodal stack (GSR + HRV + eye-tracking + fNIRS) becoming standard for ecological validity.

> **BAAS dissociation (Nature Communications 2025, DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-61706-0):** Neural affective arousal (cortical-subcortical signature: prefrontal, periaqueductal gray, thalamo-amygdala-insula) is statistically separable from autonomic arousal (GSR, HRV) and from wakefulness, validated across 24 studies, n=868. GSR and HRV index physiological/sympathetic activation; they are NOT full proxies for subjective affective arousal. Do not interpret a GSR or HRV signal as equivalent to the arousal a consumer consciously experiences. Where fMRI or EEG is unavailable, acknowledge this dissociation explicitly as a measurement limitation.

Implications:
- Engagement measured by GSR spike may reflect stress, not pleasure — and does not capture the subjective affective dimension. High autonomic arousal does not validate the experience.
- Designing a session that maintains maximum arousal throughout is designing a stress experience.
- Arousal arcs — peak then resolve — are more sustainable than flat maximum arousal.

## When to Use

- Calibrating the intensity of onboarding flows, game mechanics, or notification density.
- Diagnosing whether "high engagement" metrics reflect genuine absorption or stress-avoidance behavior.
- Designing session pacing with deliberate arousal peaks and resolution phases.
- Evaluating physiological research data (GSR, HRV, cortisol) from user studies.

## Misuse Boundary

**Ethical use**: calibrate arousal to match the complexity and value of the decision being made. Use arousal peaks to signal genuine novelty or high-value content moments. Design explicit resolution phases.

**Manipulation**: manufacturing arousal through false urgency, alarm-style notifications, manufactured scarcity signals, or escalating pressure UX to drive impulsive purchases. Any physiological research that captures GSR or HRV from users requires explicit consent; under UK GDPR, biometric physiological data is special-category data requiring Article 9 lawful basis. DMCC Act 2024 explicitly names pressure selling as actionable.

**Required condition**: arousal generated by the product must be a function of genuine content quality or real user-relevant stakes, not manufactured threat.

## Inputs

- The task complexity of the user's primary goal at this product moment.
- The expected user arousal state at session entry (context: anxious vs calm audience).
- The session duration and the designed arc (peak, resolve, exit arousal level).

## Outputs

- An arousal-calibrated session design with named peak and resolution moments.
- Notification density and intensity matched to Yerkes-Dodson optimum for the target task complexity.
- A physiological-research protocol with UK GDPR Article 9 consent documentation if GSR/HRV is measured.

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| High session-time but user reports feeling drained | Sustained above-optimum arousal; no designed resolution phase | Add explicit closure moments (summary card, "you're done" beat) at Yerkes-Dodson descent |
| GSR high but conversion flat | GSR-measured arousal from stress, not excitement; user activated but threat-appraising | Reduce arousal-inducing friction elements; add reassurance signals before CTA |
| Impulsive purchases followed by high return rate | Arousal artificially elevated by false urgency; decision made under arousal, not value | Remove manufactured urgency; let purchase decision occur at lower arousal level |
| Notification open rate drops after 10 days | Repeated arousal spikes from low-value notifications; allostatic habituation | Reduce notification frequency; increase per-notification relevance; shift from bottom-up to top-down salience strategy |

## Worked Example

**Scenario**: A subscription wellness product has a free-trial onboarding that includes a 5-screen "diagnostic quiz" with animated transitions, countdown timers on each question, and a dramatic results reveal. High completion rate but low Day-3 retention.

Diagnosis: the quiz arc sustains maximum arousal from screen 1 to results. Yerkes-Dodson: the user is running a complex evaluation task (is this product right for me?) at above-optimum arousal. The arousal feels like urgency, not insight. Post-reveal emotional register is flat or deflated.

Fix:
- Remove countdown timers from low-stakes questions; reserve time pressure for one high-value question where it is genuine.
- Design a resolution beat after the reveal: "Based on your answers, here is your personalized plan." — low arousal, high perceived value.
- Measure session arousal arc via HRV data (if consent obtained) or proxy (scroll velocity, pause duration) and confirm inverted-U shape.

**Ethical check**: no countdown timers unless they reflect real-time constraints. Arousal arc must lead to genuine insight, not to an upsell at peak stress.
