# Consumer Neuroscience Formal Theory Map

## Purpose

Use this map when a neuroscience-grounded recommendation needs a source-level mechanism, ethical boundary, or distinction between observed neural response and user-welfare outcome. Neural effects do not imply ethical permission.

## Theory Areas

| Area | Formal Objects | What It Supports | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention theory (Feature Integration Theory; Itti-Koch salience maps) | Preattentive feature detection; bottom-up vs top-down attentional priority | Predicting what captures or loses attention before deliberation | Capturing attention does not confer permission to use it; attention is a finite resource |
| Psychophysiology & autonomic regulation (Yerkes-Dodson; HPA/SAM axis; allostatic load) | Inverted-U arousal-performance curve; sympathetic activation; GSR/HRV as physiological markers | Calibrating engagement intensity; diagnosing stress cost on users | Arousal is costly; sustained high arousal without resolution is harm, not engagement |
| Social neuroendocrinology (oxytocin / affiliative circuits; Carter; Zak) | Oxytocin release via social cues (eye contact, touch, narrative); affiliative bias; prosocial motivation | Designing genuine warmth and affiliative mechanics | Oxytocin response does not verify content accuracy; warmth can be artificially induced; plasma half-life ~3–5 min. **Replication-null caveat**: the trust-causal claim (Kosfeld 2005/Zak 2012) was not supported by a high-powered registered replication (Declerck et al. 2020, Nature Human Behaviour, >95% power, no main effect; selective effect in low-trust-disposition individuals only). Treat as affiliative-signal lever, not validated trust lever. |
| Narrative cognition & Default Mode Network (Green & Brock transportation theory; Buckner DMN; vmPFC self-referential) | Transportation score as suppressor of critical evaluation; vmPFC personal-relevance encoding; ventral striatum reward tagging | Creating self-referential, immersive, emotionally resonant content | Transportation suppresses counterarguing; inserting material terms during high-immersion is manipulation |
| Regulatory focus theory & BIS/BAS (Higgins; Carver & White Gray) | Promotion-focus (BAS: approach, reward sensitivity) vs prevention-focus (BIS: avoidance, threat sensitivity) | Matching copy tone to regulatory orientation of the audience | Mismatch between funnel tone and user orientation reduces trust; not only conversion |
| Mirror neuron system & emotional contagion (Rizzolatti; Hatfield; FFA) | Motor simulation of observed actions; automatic emotional mirroring via fusiform face area | Social simulation from testimonials, faces, and emotional UGC | Mirror simulation is automatic; fabricating emotional cues triggers real neural warmth that is not earned |
| Neuroaesthetics (Ramachandran peak-shift; Chatterjee; visual reward circuitry) | Peak-shift principle; symmetry preference; contour completion; ventral pathway reward from beauty | Explaining and predicting visual aesthetic preference and beauty-driven reward | Aesthetic reward is real but does not endorse the product; beauty creates credibility debt if product underdelivers |
| Interoception & somatic marker theory (Craig insular cortex; Damasio vmPFC somatic marker hypothesis) | Insular cortex encodes body-state; vmPFC integrates somatic signal into decision bias; somatic markers precede and bias deliberate choice | Accounting for body-state signals in purchase and risk decisions | Somatic signals are pre-deliberative; manufacturing somatic anxiety to drive urgency is manipulation |
| Systems memory consolidation & sleep-dependent replay (Hebb; hippocampal-neocortical transfer; Walker sleep-memory) | Hebbian potentiation; hippocampal sharp-wave replay during NREM sleep; sleep-dependent consolidation of episodic and procedural memory | Designing for durable memory traces, not just exposure | Interruptive notifications during consolidation windows can fragment encoding; sleep is not an engagement channel |
| Incentive salience (Berridge mesolimbic dopamine; wanting vs liking dissociation; VTA; nucleus accumbens) | Anticipatory dopamine (wanting) dissociable from hedonic response (liking); VTA dopamine onset ~200ms before cue; nucleus accumbens incentive salience | Designing anticipatory mechanics (unlocks, reveals, countdowns) | Wanting loops can persist independent of liking; dissociation is the compulsion-design risk |
| Embodied / grounded cognition (Lakoff & Johnson conceptual metaphor; Barsalou perceptual symbol systems) | Sensorimotor grounding of abstract concepts; body-state metaphors in language; proprioceptive priming effects | Aligning copy and UI metaphors with actual sensorimotor product experience | Incongruent embodied metaphors create cognitive interference; do not apply random metaphors for tone |
| Predictive processing & active inference (Friston free energy principle; Clark; Constant, Friston, Kirchhoff) | Hierarchical Bayesian generative model; prediction-error minimization; active inference as action-perception loop | Managing prediction-error budget: when to confirm, when to earn a surprise | Prediction error is attentionally costly; violations without payoff degrade trust; active inference = design shapes user world-models |

## Production Rule

Every neural design lever must pass three gates before shipping: (1) the signal is true — arousal, warmth, anticipation, and somatic cues must reflect real product or social data, not manufactured stimuli; (2) user-benefit alignment — the neural response must steer the user toward an outcome they would endorse on reflection, not toward operator revenue at user expense; (3) easy reversal — every designed neural trigger must have a visible, frictionless off-path (notification off, cancel, opt-out). If the technique requires concealment of mechanism to work, it fails gate 1. If it requires complexity to exit, it fails gate 3. The DMCC Act 2024 (in force 6 April 2025) gives the CMA direct enforcement power over gate failures; fines up to 10% of global annual turnover.
