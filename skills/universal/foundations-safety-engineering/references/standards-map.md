# Standards map (pointers, not compliance claims)

Use these to borrow vocabulary and structure. Naming a standard does not show conformance to it. A conformance claim needs the full text, an assessment, and the authority named in the standard. Editions change: before citing one, look up its current edition and status on the publisher's page (iso.org, the UL Standards shop) and cite that edition.

| Standard | Scope | Useful idea | Where to confirm status |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISO 21448 (SOTIF) | Road vehicles: safety of the intended functionality | Hazards can arise with no fault present. The cause is a *functional insufficiency*: an insufficient specification, or a performance insufficiency such as a perception limit. Analyze triggering conditions alongside these insufficiencies. This is the closest standard analogue to ML or LLM errors. | ISO catalogue page for ISO 21448. The definitions above were read in a publisher sample of the standard. |
| ISO/PAS 8800 | Road vehicles: safety and artificial intelligence | Safety lifecycle for AI elements. Extends ISO 26262 and ISO 21448. | ISO catalogue page for ISO/PAS 8800. The scope above comes from a secondary source (a UL Solutions article), because iso.org could not be read. |
| UL 4600 | Safety of autonomous products | A safety-case-based standard for autonomous products. Pair with the [safety cases reference](safety-cases.md). | UL Standards shop page for UL 4600: current edition and any open revision proposals. |
| ISO 26262 / IEC 61508 | Functional safety of E/E systems | Hazards caused by faults and malfunctions. SOTIF and PAS 8800 cover what these standards leave out. | ISO and IEC catalogue pages. Not checked for this file. |

Decision rule: when a standard applies by regulation or contract, route the conformance work to its owner and keep this skill to the analysis. When no standard applies, use the SOTIF idea of "insufficiency with no fault" and the UL 4600 idea of a safety case as structure, and say that you are borrowing them.
