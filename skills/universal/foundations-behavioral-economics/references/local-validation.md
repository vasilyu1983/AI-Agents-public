# Local Behavioral Validation

Use before forecasting or deploying a behavioral intervention. Published effects motivate hypotheses; they do not determine your completion-rate multiplier or establish a neural mechanism.

## Minimal record

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Goal | User-endorsed behavior and welfare outcome, not engagement alone |
| Hypothesis | Named primitive, competing explanations and population/context limits |
| Assignment/exposure | Randomization unit, eligibility, exposure definition and denominator |
| Outcome | Baseline, outcome scale, practical effect threshold and follow-up window |
| Analysis | Appropriate uncertainty method, stopping rule and harm/override measures |
| Decision | Supported / conditional / insufficient; evidence and remaining limitations |

Choose effect metrics for the outcome. A standardized mean difference d cannot be silently converted to a rate ratio; do not forecast doubling from d=.65. Route statistical design/estimation to the statistics or causal-inference owner, and instrument validity to measurement theory.

## Habit diagnostic

Repeated use may be goal-directed, reminder-driven, socially required or automatically cued. Record cue exposure and behavior, ask about perceived automaticity using a fit-for-purpose instrument, and examine behavior when motivation or context changes without coercing users. Stable frequency, a streak or week 6–10 alone is not proof of habit. Cue dependence itself also needs alternative explanations checked.

Visible controls can act as external memory. Audit what must be remembered, grouping, expertise, search cost and observed errors before staging a flow; do not apply a four-control rule from Cowan.

## Worked case

A weekly-review plan is tested against an equally clear reminder with the same content. Randomize eligible users, measure completed reviews and perceived value, and preserve opt-out. Report baseline and interval for the chosen effect metric. Completion differences do not prove automaticity or establish the plan’s neural pathway.

## Regression checks

- d=.65 does not imply completion doubles or triples.
- Eighteen visible toggles do not by themselves violate working-memory capacity.
- Daily opens for eight weeks do not establish habitual automaticity.
- User welfare and reversal remain measured even if conversion improves.

## Primary sources

- [Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1): 94 independent tests, standardized mean effect d=.65; not a universal rate multiplier.
- [Cowan (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922): short-term memory capacity under specified task conditions, not a maximum visible-control count.
- [Wood, Tam & Witt (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.88.6.918): habits under changed circumstances; test applicability to the task.
