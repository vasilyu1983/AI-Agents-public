# Consumer Neuroscience Formal Theory Map

## Purpose

Use this map when a neuroscience-grounded recommendation needs a source-level mechanism, ethical boundary, or distinction between observed response and user-welfare outcome. Neural findings are background: they do not license a design move (no reverse inference, Poldrack 2011), and no effect implies ethical permission.

## Theory Areas

| Area | Formal Objects | What It Supports | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attention theory (Feature Integration Theory; Itti-Koch salience maps) | Preattentive feature detection; bottom-up vs top-down attentional priority | Predicting what captures or loses attention before deliberation | Capturing attention does not confer permission to use it; attention is a finite resource |
| Psychophysiology & autonomic regulation (Yerkes-Dodson; HPA/SAM axis; allostatic load) | Inverted-U arousal-performance curve; sympathetic activation; GSR/HRV as physiological markers | Calibrating engagement intensity; diagnosing stress cost on users | Arousal is costly; sustained high arousal without resolution is harm, not engagement |
| Social neuroendocrinology (oxytocin / affiliative circuits; Carter; Zak) | Oxytocin release via social cues (eye contact, touch, narrative); affiliative bias; prosocial motivation | Background only; design uses behavioural trust measures | **Replication-null**: the trust-causal claim (Kosfeld 2005; Zak 2012) was not supported by a registered replication (Declerck et al. 2020, Nature Human Behaviour 4(6):646–655; no main effect; a low-trust subgroup result was exploratory). Oxytocin is not a design lever; warmth claims must be operationally true. |
| Narrative transportation (Green & Brock 2000; van Laer et al. 2014 meta-analysis) | Self-reported transportation; reduced counterarguing; story-consistent attitudes | Creating immersive, personally relevant content, measured with the transportation scale | Transportation suppresses counterarguing; inserting material terms during high-immersion is manipulation |
| Regulatory focus & fit (Higgins 1997, 2000; Motyka et al. 2014) | Promotion (gains, ideals) vs prevention (safety, duties); fit between frame and active goal raises value | Matching message frame to the goal the decision serves | BIS/BAS (Carver & White 1994) is a distinct construct, not promotion/prevention; fit effects vary by moderators; never classify users by channel |
| Mirror neuron system & emotional contagion (Rizzolatti; Hatfield; FFA) | Research on action observation and emotional responses; no automatic interface-to-circuit inference | Behavioral hypotheses about perceived authenticity and emotional congruence in social imagery | Misrepresented testimonials can deceive users; no inevitable neural warmth or simulation effect follows from imagery alone |
| Neuroaesthetics (Ramachandran peak-shift; Chatterjee; visual reward circuitry) | Peak-shift principle; symmetry preference; contour completion; ventral pathway reward from beauty | Explaining and predicting visual aesthetic preference and beauty-driven reward | Aesthetic reward is real but does not endorse the product; beauty creates credibility debt if product underdelivers |
| Interoception & felt state (Craig 2009; somatic marker hypothesis contested: Dunn, Dalgleish & Lawrence 2006, Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev. 30(2):239–271) | Self-reported felt state; affect used as information in judgement | Acknowledging users' stated feelings in purchase and risk decisions | Insula activity is non-selective; manufacturing body-state alarm to drive urgency is manipulation |
| Memory: spacing and retrieval practice (behavioural); sleep consolidation as background | Spaced repetition; retrieval practice; delayed recall | Designing for durable recall, measured by delayed recall and retention | No product-level "consolidation window" exists; respect sleep and quiet hours for welfare, not as a memory lever |
| Incentive salience (Berridge mesolimbic dopamine; wanting vs liking dissociation; VTA; nucleus accumbens) | Wanting dissociable from liking (animal evidence; Berridge); in products, measured as anticipation vs satisfaction self-report | Designing anticipatory mechanics (unlocks, reveals, countdowns) | Wanting loops can persist independent of liking; dissociation is the compulsion-design risk |
| Embodied / grounded cognition (Lakoff & Johnson conceptual metaphor; Barsalou perceptual symbol systems) | Sensorimotor grounding of abstract concepts; body-state metaphors in language; proprioceptive priming effects | Aligning copy and UI metaphors with actual sensorimotor product experience | Incongruent embodied metaphors create cognitive interference; do not apply random metaphors for tone |
| Expectation and predictability (predictive processing as background only — Clark 2013) | Learned expectations; expectancy violation (N400 in lab) | Keeping routine flows consistent; announcing changes | No quantified "prediction-error budget" exists; measure errors and task time after changes |

## Production Rule

Every design lever must pass three gates before shipping: (1) the signal is true — urgency, warmth, anticipation and social-proof cues must reflect real product or social data, not manufactured stimuli; (2) user-benefit alignment — the design must steer the user toward an outcome they would endorse on reflection, not toward operator revenue at user expense; (3) easy reversal — every designed trigger must have a visible, frictionless off-path (notification off, cancel, opt-out). If the technique requires concealment of mechanism to work, it fails gate 1. If it requires complexity to exit, it fails gate 3.
