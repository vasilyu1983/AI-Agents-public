# Primitive: Memory Consolidation & Sleep

## Definition

Consolidation stabilizes and integrates memories over time. Sleep can support memory retention, and human intracranial recordings provide evidence of cortical replay during NREM sleep. Replay also occurs during wakefulness. These mechanisms do not establish a universal local-clock notification window, a best post-wake minute, or a retention benefit for a particular app.

Hebbian plasticity is a cellular learning principle, not evidence that a repeated notification schedule has formed a product habit. Distinguish mechanism evidence, a validated task measure, and an intervention effect.

## When to Use

- Designing reminders that respect sleep and user preferences.
- Testing recall or learning outcomes when consolidation is a plausible mechanism.
- Separating notification open rates, memory retention and sustained product value.

## Misuse Boundary

Do not interrupt sleep to optimize opens or infer sleep schedules from unrelated personal data. Let users choose timing, quiet hours and withdrawal. A locally chosen reminder cadence is a product default, not a biological requirement.

## Inputs

- User-selected timezone, preferred cue and quiet hours.
- Relevant task and outcome: recall, completion or user-reported value.
- Baseline, eligible population, alternative behavioral explanations and experiment budget.

## Outputs

- Preference-respecting reminder specification.
- Randomized eligible timing comparison with predeclared outcomes and uncertainty.
- Separate mechanism, measurement and product-effect evidence; mark missing evidence explicitly.

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Possible explanations | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Opens rise but completion does not | Irrelevant content, friction or cue mismatch | Test relevance and task entry; do not diagnose failed consolidation |
| Users forget the task | Cue mismatch, low value or memory demands | Assess the actual task and user-selected cue |
| Night reminders cause complaints | Sleep interruption or inappropriate timing | Honor quiet hours; investigate harm before further timing tests |
| Stable daily use is called a neural habit | Motivation, reminders or goal-directed behavior can explain use | Measure automaticity and cue dependence separately |

## Worked Example

A daily reading app tests one optional reminder at a user-selected eligible time against its existing reminder policy. Randomize users, respect quiet hours in both arms, hold content constant and compare task completion, perceived value, opt-outs and sleep-interruption reports. Predeclare the useful effect and stopping rule. A retention difference establishes neither replay nor a dopamine mechanism. Do not attribute a multi-change before/after result to consolidation.

## Sources

- Hebb (1949), *The Organization of Behavior*: foundational plasticity theory; not an app-timing trial.
- Jiang et al. (2017), “Replay of large-scale spatio-temporal patterns from waking during subsequent NREM sleep in human cortex,” [primary human recording study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29234075/). Supports NREM replay, not fixed evening notification windows.
- Walker (2017), *Why We Sleep*: secondary overview; use primary task-specific evidence for intervention claims.
