# Primitive: Narrative Transportation

## Definition

Narrative transportation is the state of cognitive and emotional immersion in a story. Green and Brock (2000) established transportation as a measurable psychological construct (the transportation scale). It predicts story-consistent beliefs and attitudes over and above argument processing. van Laer et al. (2014, _Journal of Consumer Research_ 40(5):797–817) meta-analysed two decades of consumer research on its antecedents and consequences. In their account, transportation is driven by empathy for the characters and mental imagery of the plot.

Neural background, which is context only and not a design lever: self-referential and narrative processing has been associated with default-mode regions (Buckner et al. 2008). Those regions serve many functions, including mind-wandering. Activation there does not show that a given piece of content transported anyone. Measure transportation with the validated self-report scale and behavioural outcomes (recall, attitude, saves, shares). Do not infer it from a region label.

Implications:
- Transportation can reduce counterarguing, so a transported reader may scrutinise claims less. This makes narrative powerful and ethically loaded.
- Grammatical person and tense (second-person present vs first-person past) are copy variables to test. No person or tense is biologically required for self-relevant processing.
- Test whether a narrative format beats a factual format on the outcome you care about. Do not assume it does.

## When to Use

- Designing personalized readings, interpretive content, or any experience that must feel authored for the individual.
- Crafting product origin stories or "person like me" testimonials where identification is the mechanism.
- Building onboarding narratives whose recall and attitude effects you will measure.
- Evaluating whether data-targeted "personal" content is actually experienced as personal (transportation scale, perceived relevance), not assuming it is.

## Misuse Boundary

**Ethical use**: narrative immersion that serves the user's accurate understanding of the product or their own situation. Transportation into a true story is legitimate even when emotionally powerful.

**Manipulation**: using reduced counterarguing during immersion to deliver inaccurate product claims, false testimonials, or misleading pricing framing inside a narrative arc. Material disclosures inserted during high-transportation states may receive less scrutiny — this exploits immersion and is inconsistent with DMCC Act 2024 transparency requirements. Under ASA CAP Code, content presented as personal or scientific that is neither is a misleading claim.

**Required condition**: all factual claims within narrative content must be accurate. Interpretive or predictive content (horoscopes, tarot, compatibility readings) must be labeled as such. Transportation cannot be used to insert material contract terms or pricing conditions that the user will later dispute.

## Inputs

- The narrative frame (first-person past-tense "person like me" story, or second-person present-tense "you" address).
- User-provided data or context that enables genuine personalization (name, zodiac sign, quiz answers, stated preferences).
- The accuracy and labeling of the content being delivered.

## Outputs

- A content structure that opens with an orienting narrative, with person/tense variants set up as a copy test.
- Personalization hooks that connect user-provided data to the narrative arc at key emotional moments.
- A disclosure label for interpretive/predictive content that does not break the narrative arc but establishes the content category.

## Failure Modes

| Failure | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Content is data-targeted but feels generic | Generic wording; low perceived relevance; low transportation score | Test a second-person variant and a named user-data hook in the first two sentences; measure perceived relevance |
| High read-through rate but low save or share rate | Content was read but lacked an emotional peak or a reason to share | Add one emotionally resonant moment at the narrative arc peak; connect it to a user-stated desire or concern |
| User disputes claim they "agreed to" during purchase | Material term presented inside a high-immersion narrative, where it received less scrutiny | Move all material disclosures (price, T&C, renewal terms) to pre-narrative or post-narrative, low-load screens |
| High-narrative content feels manipulative in qualitative feedback | Immersive framing but content was inaccurate or the "personal" framing was generic data masquerade | Ensure actual personalization accuracy; label interpretive content honestly |

## Worked Example

**Scenario**: A tarot subscription app shows users a daily card with a generic interpretation. Save rate is 4%; share rate is 1.8%. Sessions are long but cohort drop-off at Week 4 is 55%.

Hypothesis: the interpretation is third-person and factual ("The Hermit card indicates introspection..."), so users may not experience it as personal. This is a hypothesis about perceived relevance and transportation, to be tested; it is not a neural diagnosis.

Fix:
1. Reframe to second-person present-tense: "Right now, you're in a passage where stillness serves you better than motion."
2. Add a user-data hook: "Based on your Moon in Scorpio, this transit lands differently for you than for others born under air signs."
3. A/B test the rewrite against the original on save rate, share rate and a two-item perceived-relevance/transportation check; keep a Week-4 retention guardrail.

**Ethical check**: the interpretation is labeled "tarot interpretation" not "prediction" or "psychological diagnosis." The user provided their birth data voluntarily. No material claims are embedded in the narrative arc.
